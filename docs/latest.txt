A digital bridge between brain and spine gave a paralyzed man voluntary movement again — a decade after his injury.

Researchers at EPFL implanted electrodes in the brain and spinal cord, creating a real-time wireless link that bypasses the injury site. The participant, paralyzed for 10 years after a cycling accident, regained controlled walking within the first training session.

The system uses predictive AI to decode motor intent and deliver precise epidural stimulation below the lesion — no manual calibration required during use.

This is not a cure. It is a proof-of-concept with n=1. Larger trials are ongoing.
