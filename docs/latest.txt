What if your brain could talk to your legs again — wirelessly?

Researchers in Switzerland developed a brain–spine interface that translates thought into movement. A man paralyzed for over a decade was able to walk overground using this system.

This isn't a cure. But it is a signal — that the nervous system retains more potential than we once believed, and that science is listening.

🔬 What the research showed:
→ A wireless implant decoded motor intentions from the brain
→ Targeted electrical stimulation activated leg muscles below the injury
→ With training, some neurological recovery was observed even without the device

We share this because hope grounded in evidence matters. For the 18,000+ people who sustain a spinal cord injury in the U.S. each year, progress like this is personal.

Follow along as we track the science — and the people behind it.
