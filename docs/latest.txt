Electrical stimulation below a spinal injury can wake up circuits once thought permanently silent.

A landmark study from the University of Louisville applied continuous epidural electrical stimulation (EES) to 9 individuals with complete motor paralysis. All 9 regained voluntary leg movement — some within the first week of stimulation.

Critically, 3 participants retained voluntary control even when the device was off, suggesting real neurological reorganization, not just device-dependent output.

This builds on the 2018 Nature Medicine cohort and advances the case for EES as a clinical rehabilitation tool — not a theoretical one.
