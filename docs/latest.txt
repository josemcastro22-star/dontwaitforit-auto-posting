Electrical stimulation below a spinal injury can wake up circuits once thought permanently silent.

Researchers at University of Louisville delivered continuous epidural electrical stimulation (EES) to 9 individuals with complete motor paralysis. All 9 regained voluntary leg movement during stimulation — and some retained function after it ended.

The spinal cord contains neural networks capable of reorganization. EES appears to amplify residual signals the brain is still sending, even when clinical tests show no detectable motor function.

This does not mean a cure. It means the biology is more recoverable than standard diagnosis suggests — and that timing and protocol matter.
