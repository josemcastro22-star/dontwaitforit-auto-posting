A digital bridge between brain and spine let a man with complete paralysis walk again — and the gains outlasted the device itself.

Researchers at EPFL implanted electrodes in the brain and spinal cord, creating a real-time wireless link that decoded motor intent and delivered targeted stimulation below the injury.

Participant Gert-Jan Oskam regained voluntary leg control after 12 years of paralysis. After months of rehab with the system, neurological recovery continued even without stimulation — suggesting the interface may promote nerve regeneration.

This is early-stage. One participant. But the mechanism matters.
